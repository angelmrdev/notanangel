**Your first complete mix does not need effects, tricks or a complex EQ routine.** It needs two suitable tracks, a reliable entry, stable timing and a controlled changeover. This capstone lesson brings together everything you practised in Module 06 to create a simple, repeatable transition.

## Learning objectives

By the end, you can:

1. Choose a pair of tracks and a **realistic overlap** based on their arrangements.
2. Prepare Deck B in headphone CUE while Deck A stays on MASTER.
3. Launch B on a suitable beat and **phrase boundary**, then keep it aligned.
4. Transfer the audience from A to B **without a jump in loudness** or a messy low-end overlap.
5. Review your attempt and identify one concrete adjustment for the next run.

## 1. Pick an easy first transition

Start with two steady 4/4 tracks with similar playable BPM, clear kick drums and predictable eight- or sixteen-bar sections. An instrumental intro on B and a less-busy outro or breakdown on A make the first attempts easier. **Do not assume that two tracks at the same BPM fit musically.** Check vocals, bass, mood and energy by ear.

| Check | Beginner-friendly choice | Red flag |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tempo | Close BPM; both hold a steady pulse | Large mismatch or variable live timing |
| Phrase | Clear downbeats and sections | Uncertain bar one or irregular arrangement |
| Arrangement | B intro against A outro / sparse section | Two lead vocals competing |
| Low end | One dominant bass part at a time | Both full basslines fighting |
| Level | Similar perceived loudness at safe headroom | Clip indicators or sudden volume jumps |

For a first attempt, try a **short, controlled 8- or 16-bar transition** rather than forcing a long blend. Long blends are useful only when the arrangements support them.

## 2. Plan the moment when the songs meet

Find the first beat of the section where A should hand over to B. Listen to B's intro and count its length: if B takes eight bars to reach its main section, starting it eight bars before the corresponding change in A can produce a natural handover. This is a *planning rule*, not a guarantee that the tracks will sound good together.

<EntryPlannerEn />

Count along without staring at the beatgrid: **1-2-3-4** for each bar, then group bars into phrases. If the entry is late, stop and restart B while its channel is still closed. An incorrect phrase entry cannot always be repaired by nudging a jog wheel.

## 3. Prepare Deck B privately

With A playing to the room, load B, keep **B's channel fader down**, enable B's headphone CUE and check the cue/master balance. Set the start CUE at the correct downbeat. Match the tempos sufficiently for the intended overlap and listen for drift over several bars. If B slips progressively, fix its rate before worrying about the perfect launch. If the offset remains fixed, adjust its position.

Before opening B, verify:

- B is audible **only in headphones**, not accidentally over the room speakers.
- Its BPM and beat timing remain usable over the overlap.
- Its intended first beat and phrase position are identified.
- The master output and individual channel levels have safe headroom; no clipping.
- You know which parts of both tracks will overlap, particularly vocals and bass.

These steps build on [How to Cue a Track in DJ Headphones](/learn-to-dj/beatmatching/how-to-cue-dj-tracks) and [How to Fix Beatmatching Drift](/learn-to-dj/beatmatching/fix-beatmatching-drift).

## 4. Perform a simple blend

**Stage A — Prepare.** Keep A at its normal, correctly gain-staged level. B remains closed. Listen to B privately, then trigger it at the phrase entry you counted.

**Stage B — Blend.** Confirm beats and phrases hold together, then raise B's channel slowly while monitoring MASTER. Avoid piling two heavy basslines on top of each other; where necessary, reduce the incoming track's lows temporarily and bring them in as the outgoing bass recedes. EQ positions vary by musical material and mixer: there is no universal knob setting.

**Stage C — Handover.** As B reaches the intended musical section, lower A smoothly, return B's sound to an appropriate balance and close A completely. The goal is a seamless musical change, not necessarily equal channel-fader positions.

The simulator illustrates **relative positions**, not live audio or measured gain. In a real mix, two open faders do not simply equal a fixed output percentage: frequencies overlap, perceived loudness varies and clipping is possible.

## 5. Practice on two real decks

<FinalDrillEn />

After each take, listen back or record a short practice segment **if you have permission to record the material**. Make one change at a time. A tiny improvement in phrase choice may matter more than another EQ trick.

## 6. Troubleshooting the first transition

| What you notice | Likely cause | Next attempt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kicks separate more as you blend | Playback tempos differ | Re-check B's tempo and nudge only after fixing drift |
| Beats align, but the chorus enters at an awkward moment | Phrase mismatch | Count sections again and move B's launch point |
| Bass turns muddy or boomy | Low-frequency overlap | Reduce overlapping lows and check gain staging |
| Output suddenly gets much louder | Two energetic sections stacked; gain too high | Lower levels, simplify overlap and monitor meters |
| B arrives too quietly | Channel too low or source mastered differently | Compare perceived level during preparation; don't chase 0 dB |
| Two vocals clash | Poor arrangement pairing | Use an instrumental passage or shorten the overlap |
| You freeze while mixing | Too many tasks at once | Rehearse the same two tracks using one simple transition |

## 7. Self-assessment: are you ready for the next module?

- Can you describe **where** the next track should start and **why**?
- Can you prepare B in headphones without leaking it to MASTER?
- Can you keep the beats together for the whole overlap without trusting Sync blindly?
- Can you close A and leave B at a safe, consistent listening level?
- Can you identify whether a failed attempt was caused by **timing, phrasing, arrangement or level**?

If not, repeat the same transition. You do not need to rush into effects. **One clean, understandable handover is the milestone.** The earlier lessons on [beatmatching by ear](/learn-to-dj/beatmatching/how-to-beatmatch-by-ear), [aligning beats and phrases](/learn-to-dj/beatmatching/align-beats-and-phrases) and [BPM Sync vs Beat Sync](/learn-to-dj/beatmatching/bpm-sync-vs-beat-sync) are available whenever you need targeted revision.
