**Pre-listening is the difference between practising a launch and accidentally playing it to the room.** In this lesson, Deck A is already playing to the audience; you use headphones to prepare Deck B, listen to its start and choose the right moment to enter. We are applying the timing skills from [aligning beats, bars and phrases](/learn-to-dj/beatmatching/align-beats-and-phrases), not repeating the physical connections from Module 03.

## Learning objectives

By the end you can:

1. Distinguish **headphones CUE** (private monitoring) from a saved **cue point** (playback location).
2. Check that B is audible to you but not to the master output.
3. Prepare a useful downbeat and rehearse a launch safely.
4. Choose an entry in A's count and listen for short offsets versus continuing drift.
5. Know when B is truly ready to enter the public mix.

## 1. Two meanings of CUE

A **headphones CUE** button routes a channel into the headphones monitoring path, typically before the channel fader. A **cue point** is a location in a track you can return to and launch from. A **hot cue** often provides direct recall and playback at a stored position, depending on your software and settings. They solve different problems: **what you hear privately** and **where playback starts**.

| Control | Purpose | Typical beginner mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channel CUE / PFL | Privately monitor Deck B | Assuming this opens B to the audience |
| Channel fader / crossfader | Determine what reaches the master | Opening B before checking its timing |
| CUE or temporary cue point | Prepare a repeatable start | Setting it on the first visible kick without counting |
| Hot cue | Recall a saved start quickly | Assuming all platforms launch a hot cue in exactly the same way |
| Headphones CUE/MASTER mix | Blend private and live reference | Turning fully to MASTER and wondering where B went |

## 2. Set up a private listening path

With A already audible on master, select B's headphone CUE while **keeping B's channel out of master**. With typical pre-fader monitoring, you can hear B in headphones even when B's channel fader is down. The audience does not hear B until you open an appropriate master route.

<CueRoutingLabEn />

Actual routing varies by controllers, mixer settings, crossfader assignments and software. Check both your ears and the output meters rather than assuming one layout is universal. Keep headphones at a comfortable level; louder monitoring does not make a bad cue point accurate.

## 3. Find the start you actually want

Listen for the **downbeat of the chosen bar**, not merely a loud transient. Count **1–2–3–4** and find an intro, percussion entrance or clean segment where B can start without colliding with A's vocal. If the track starts with silence, a pickup note or an irregular phrase, the first waveform peak may not be the best launch point.

Set a temporary cue point or hot cue at that position, then rehearse starting B while it remains private. Use pause/cue/play behavior appropriate to your hardware: some controls return to a temporary cue; others launch or trigger a stored hot cue. Test the behavior before relying on it in a mix.

## 4. Launch B against A's count

Listen to A's bar and phrase position. Decide **which section of A** should meet **which beginning of B**. A correctly prepared cue point is only half the timing decision; you still need to launch at the planned count.

<LaunchTimingLabEn />

The diagram checks *bar placement only*. A start on beat 1 is not automatically a good musical transition; phrases and vocal space still matter. For a small fixed error after launch, nudge or restart if appropriate. If the two tracks increasingly drift apart, recheck tempo instead of nudging continuously.

## 5. Monitor privately before opening the channel

While B is in headphones, compare its kick or snare with A in the CUE/MASTER blend, or use one ear to hear the room if your setup allows. Listen over more than one bar. Check that B is not being broadcast, that its level is appropriate and that the entry fits the arrangement.

Once confident, bring B into master progressively. This is a **performance decision**, not the same action as pressing CUE. Watch levels and avoid sudden high-volume entries. Later lessons will cover the full EQ and transition strategy.

## 7. Troubleshooting

| What happens | Check first |
| --- | --- |
| You hear A but not B in headphones | B's CUE selection, headphone blend, cue volume and audio route |
| You hear B in the room while preparing | Channel fader, crossfader assignment, routing or another open source |
| B starts late every attempt | Preparation point, transport response, count and launch timing |
| B sounds aligned initially but drifts | Tempo relationship, variable tempo or analysis |
| Kick drums align but vocals clash | Phrase selection and arrangement; don't fix with a jog nudge |
| Headphones are too loud or distorted | Reduce level and inspect gain staging before continuing |

## Self-check

1. Can B play privately in headphones with its channel fader down?
2. Does activating headphones CUE automatically make B audible on master?
3. What is the difference between channel CUE and a saved cue point?
4. If B starts on beat 2 of A, does equal BPM solve the bar misalignment?
5. What should you verify before raising B's channel fader?

**Answers:** (1) Yes, with appropriate pre-fader routing. (2) No. (3) Monitoring route versus track position. (4) No; choose the correct launch count. (5) Headphone and master routing, timing, musical fit and levels.

## Key takeaway

**Prepare in private, launch with intention, verify by ear, then open the channel.** Good pre-listening buys you time to correct mistakes before anybody else hears them.
