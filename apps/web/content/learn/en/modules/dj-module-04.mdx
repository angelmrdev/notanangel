**DJ Software** explains what happens between choosing a track and hearing it from the speakers. You will learn the shared concepts behind DJ applications without relying on a particular brand, price tier or software version.

## Learning outcomes

- Follow the route from a local or supported streaming source to playback and audio outputs.
- Recognize decks, waveforms, browser and collection views.
- Configure audio device, master and cue routing, and understand latency trade-offs.
- Interpret track analysis while checking suspicious BPM, key and beatgrid results.
- Distinguish preparation, performance and hardware integration modes.
- Evaluate compatibility and rehearse a reliable offline workflow.

## Recommended sequence

Study the seven lessons in order. Keep your current DJ application's settings and manual available for comparison, but use generic concepts as the reference. Some features depend on hardware, licence, track source and version.

<PracticeExercise title="Your software inventory">Note which DJ application you use, your operating system, the audio device shown in its preferences and where your tracks are stored. Do not change working audio settings before recording their original values.</PracticeExercise>

## Safety and scope

Start practice with master and headphones at low volume. Only use media you have permission to play and obey any restrictions imposed by streaming services. A streamed track may not be available for offline export, recording or standalone playback.
